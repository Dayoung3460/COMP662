## What is an Autoencoder?

- A neural network that learns to encode data into the latent representation, and reconstruct the original data from that representation.
- It consists of an encoder, a latent representation, and a decoder.
- Encoder: maps the input into (generally lower) latent dimensions.
- Latent representation: Learned feature representation.
- Decoder: Reconstructs the input from the latent representation.

---

## Autoencoder Reconstruction Objective

- Input $\mathbf{x}$
- Latent representation $\mathbf{z} = f_E(\mathbf{x})$
- Output $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = f_D(\mathbf{z})$
- Reconstruction loss: $\mathcal{L}_{AE} = ||\mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}||^2$

---

## Autoencoder Architecture

```
Input → [Encoder] → Latent Space (Bottleneck) → [Decoder] → Reconstruction
  x                                                              x̂
```

Encoder narrows the input down to a compact latent space; the decoder expands it back out to reconstruct the original input (e.g., an image of the digit "3" in, reconstructed "3" out).

---

## Autoencoder Applications

- Dimensionality reduction: similar to PCA
- Denoising: e.g., noisy images
- Anomaly detection: reconstruction error
- Representation learning: learning features

---

## Is an Autoencoder Good at Generation?

- Deterministic encoding and decoding, no uncertainty modeling.
- Latent space can be irregular and discontinuous.
- Sampling of the latent space is generally not meaningful.
- Latent representation interpolation may fail.
- May memorise the training data.

---

## What is Wrong with Random Sampling?

- Decoder only sees valid latent representations from the training data.
- Randomly sampled latent representations may fall into invalid regions.

---

## Desired Properties of a Generative Latent Space

- Smoothness
- Continuity
- Meaningful sampling
- Interpolation capacity

---

## What is a Variational Autoencoder?

- Does not encode a single latent vector.
- Instead, it encodes a probability distribution.
- The latent representation $\mathbf{z}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$.
- The probability density function (pdf) of a normal/Gaussian distribution is

$$f_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{z}_i) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left(-\frac{(\mathbf{z}_i - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$

- The latent representation is sampled from the predicted distribution.

---

## Why the Probabilistic Latent Space?

- The probabilistic representation spreads information (features) across regions instead of isolated points.
- Smooth, continuous, and meaningful.
- Captures uncertainty.
- Better generalisation, prevents memorisation.

---

## Why Gaussian Distribution?

- Simple to use.
- Closed-form solution.
- Central Limit Theorem: the averages of sufficiently large random samples from any population will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the population's underlying distribution.

---

## VAE Architecture

```
Input x → [Encoder qφ(z|x)] → μ ─────┐
                             │        + → × → z → [Decoder pθ(x|z)] → Reconstructed x̂
                             └ log σ² → exp → √ ┘        ↑
                                                  ε ~ N(0, I)
```

The encoder outputs $\mu$ and $\log \sigma^2$; $\sigma$ is combined with a random sample $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ to form the latent code $\mathbf{z}$, which the decoder turns back into a reconstruction.

---

## VAE Encoder

- Maps input into a Gaussian distribution.
- $q_\phi(\mathbf{z}_i|\mathbf{x}_i) = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$
- Outputs: mean $\mu$, variance $\sigma^2$

---

## VAE Latent Space

- Prior: $p(\mathbf{z}_i) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
- Posterior $q_\phi(\mathbf{z}_i|\mathbf{x}_i)$ is estimated by the encoder.
- Can we directly sample $\mathbf{z}_i$ from the posterior?

---

## Reparameterisation Trick

- Direct sampling of $\mathbf{z}_i$ blocks backpropagation.
- Sample $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
- Let $\mathbf{z}_i = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$.
- Differentiation with respect to $\mu$ and $\sigma$.
- Stochasticity is isolated in $\epsilon$.

---

## VAE Decoder

- Decoder output: $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = f_\theta(\mathbf{z})$.
- Probabilistic interpretation: $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i|\mathbf{z}_i) = \mathcal{N}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i, \sigma^2)$
- Reconstruction goal: $\hat{\mathbf{x}} \approx \mathbf{x}$.
- Distribution goal: $q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \approx p(\mathbf{z})$.
- Trade-off between reconstruction and distribution regularisation.

---

## Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence

- It measures how different two probability distributions are.
- VAE KL divergence term $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \,\|\, p(\mathbf{z}))$
- Small KL divergence suggests that the distributions are similar.
- KL divergence is not symmetric.

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \,\|\, p(\mathbf{z})) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(1 + \log\sigma_j^2 - \mu_j^2 - \sigma_j^2\right)$$

- Penalises large mean and variance
- Penalises deviation from standard Gaussian

---

## VAE Full Objective

- Learns both reconstruction and a smooth probabilistic latent space.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VAE}} = -\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}\left[\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\right] + D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \,\|\, p(\mathbf{z})\right)$$

- Reconstruction loss: $-\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}\left[\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\right] \propto ||\mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}||^2$

---

## Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log p_\theta(x) &= \log \int p_\theta(x, z)\, dz \\
&= \log \int q_\phi(z|x) \frac{p_\theta(x, z)}{q_\phi(z|x)}\, dz \\
&= \log \int q_\phi(z|x) \frac{p_\theta(x|z) p(z)}{q_\phi(z|x)}\, dz \\
&\geq \int q_\phi(z|x) \log \frac{p_\theta(x|z) p(z)}{q_\phi(z|x)}\, dz \\
&= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[\log p_\theta(x|z)\right] + \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[\log \frac{p(z)}{q_\phi(z|x)}\right] \\
&= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[\log p_\theta(x|z)\right] - D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p(z)\right) \\
&= \mathcal{L}_{ELBO}
\end{aligned}
$$

## VAE Advantages

- Representation learning
- Compact embeddings
- Uncertainty modeling
- Fast inference
- Stable training
- Interpolation
- Anomaly detection

---

## Summary

- Autoencoder learns latent representation, but not generative.
- VAE introduces a probabilistic latent space.
- VAE encoder predicts mean and variance.
- VAE latent representations are sampled using reparameterisation trick.
- VAE cares about both reconstruction and probability distribution.
