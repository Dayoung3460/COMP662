# Kia whai hua | Course Aims and Learning Outcomes

## Aims

The main aims of this course are:

1. Examine Generative Artificial Intelligence techniques for creating synthetic datasets in land-use modelling.
2. Investigate the applications of advanced generative models in Artificial Intelligence-driven land-use modelling.
3. Develop expertise in prompt engineering and retrieval-based generation techniques to improve model performance.

## Learning outcomes

After successfully completing this course, you will be able to:

- **LO1.** Construct Artificial Intelligence generated synthetic datasets for training land-use models.
- **LO2.** Implement advanced generative techniques to produce high-resolution satellite imagery for land management.
- **LO3.** Optimise language models for decision support and land-use scenario planning.
- **LO4.** Implement Artificial Intelligence-powered chatbots for environmental and policy advisory services.
- **LO5.** Critically evaluate the performance of Generative Artificial Intelligence models in land-use scenarios.

## Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) Usage

The use of GenAI tools is permitted in this course for **all** assessments. GenAI tools include, but are not limited to, large language models (e.g. ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity, Bard), grammar/writing software (e.g. Grammarly Premium), language translation software (e.g. Lokalise, Quillbot), coding (e.g., Qodo, GitHub Copilot), and image generating tools (e.g. DALL-E 2, Midjourney, Leonardo).

Refer to the assessment instructions, available on the Akoraka | Learn course page, for the GenAI Usage for each assessment item.

### Kaupapa Tuhika 1 | Synthetic Dataset Generation

This assessment is to be submitted by 5:00 PM on Sunday, 23 August 2026 via the Akoraka | Learn course page. The Kaupapa Tuhika 1 is to be completed individually. It contributes 20% of the final grade. You will be provided with a specific land-use modelling scenario for which you will implement a generative model to create a synthetic dataset. This will involve selecting and training an appropriate generative technique, generating synthetic outputs, and critically evaluating the quality and validity of the generated data using appropriate metrics, including downstream task performance by training models on the synthetic data. Instructions will be made available on the Akoraka | Learn course page.

### Kaupapa Tuhika 2 | Retrieval-Augmented Generation

This assessment is to be submitted by 5:00 PM on Sunday, 4 October 2026 via the Akoraka | Learn course page. The Kaupapa Tuhika 2 is to be completed individually. It contributes 30% of the final grade. You will be provided with a land-use policy scenario for which you will develop an integrated Large Language Model-based application combining Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and prompt engineering capabilities. This will involve building a system that retrieves and processes relevant policy documents using RAG techniques and applies optimised prompt engineering strategies to generate contextually appropriate responses for land-use planning and policy recommendation. You will critically assess your system's performance and limitations using appropriate evaluation metrics. Instructions will be made available on the Akoraka | Learn course page.

### Kaupapa Tuhika 3 | Generative AI Final Project

The Kaupapa Tuhika 3 is to be completed individually. It contributes 50% of the final grade. In this assessment, you will design and implement a comprehensive generative AI system that integrates multiple techniques learned throughout the course to address a real-world problem in land use, environmental monitoring, or policy analysis of your choice. This may involve combining synthetic data generation, high-resolution imagery synthesis, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), language model optimisation, and conversational AI capabilities to create an end-to-end solution. You will evaluate your system's performance using relevant metrics and critically assess its capabilities, limitations, and real-world applicability. This assessment consists of two components:

- **Proposal Submission**: 5:00 PM on Friday, 16 October 2026 (worth 10% of the final grade) - to be submitted via the Akoraka | Learn course page
- **Final Submission and Presentation**: By the date scheduled by the exams office (worth 40% of the final grade)

The **Proposal Submission** requires a detailed project proposal outlining the problem statement, proposed methodology, implementation plan with key milestones, and evaluation strategy. The **Final Submission** includes complete system implementation, comprehensive evaluation, and a detailed technical report documenting your methodology, results, and critical reflections. The **Presentation** requires you to present your solution and discuss key findings, insights, and lessons learned. Instructions will be made available on the Akoraka | Learn course page.

## Kā whakapāha | Aegrotats

Aegrotat applications may be made when due to a *serious event*: (1) a student has missed an assessment; (2) a student has completed an assessment but believes they have been impaired by one of the above circumstances; or (3) a student is seeking an extension to the due date for an assessment. *Serious events* mean illness (including COVID-19) or injury; bereavement, trauma, critical circumstances, disasters; and misadventure. To apply for an Aegrotat, your assessment needs to be worth 10% or more of your final course grade. Apply through the online portal on Akoraka | Learn which can be accessed from the link within the Exams & Assessments section on the right of your Akoraka | Learn dashboard. If your Aegrotat application is deemed justified (i.e., approved) by the Examinations Office, the Examiner will decide what the outcome will be - this may include approving an extension, offering an alternative assessment or deriving a grade. For more information on Aegrotats refer to the online portal. For assessments worth less than 10%, you should normally approach the Examiner or their nominee (such as a lecturer or tutor) directly, in person or in writing.

## Te whakaroaka I te tuku | Extensions and Late Submission of Assessment

For items of assessment worth 10% or less, you should approach the Examiner or their nominee (such as a lecturer or tutor) directly, in person or in writing. For items of assessment worth 10% or more of your final course grade, if there is a serious event that contributes to you seeking an extension or submitting a piece of assessment late, you should normally approach the Examiner (for extensions of 3 days or fewer) or apply for an Aegrotat (for extensions of more than three days). Also refer to the section on Aegrotats above. The Examiner will decide the outcome of your request - this may include approving an extension or removing any late penalties. If denied, items of assessment (other than tests) that are submitted after the due date and time will have a maximum late penalty applied as noted below. University regulations apply where a final examination is scheduled.

Items of assessment that are submitted after the due date and time will attract an immediate penalty of up to 25% of the marks available. Those received more than 48 hours after the due date and time will be awarded a mark of zero.

Items of assessment that are submitted after the due date and time, without Examiner approval, will have a maximum late penalty applied as noted below.
